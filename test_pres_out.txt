<presentation title="Boiler Safety Overview">
  <slide title="Why Boiler Safety Matters">
    - Boilers operate under high pressure and temperature, posing serious injury and property‑damage risks if not properly managed.  
    - Historical incidents (e.g., 2015 Texas boiler explosion) highlight the cost of inadequate safety controls.  
    - Regulatory compliance (ASME, OSHA, local codes) is mandatory to avoid fines and shutdowns.  
  </slide>
  <slide title="Common Hazards & Prevention Strategies">
    - **Over‑pressure**: Install and regularly test pressure relief valves; maintain set‑points per design specifications.  
    - **Low water level**: Use low‑water cutoffs and continuous level monitoring; train operators on alarm response.  
    - **Fuel/combustion issues**: Ensure proper fuel‑air mixing, regular burner inspections, and flame monitoring sensors.  
    - **Corrosion & scale**: Implement water treatment programs, schedule boiler cleaning, and monitor pH/oxygen levels.  
    - **Human error**: Enforce lock‑out/tag‑out (LOTO) procedures, provide competency‑based training, and maintain up‑to‑date SOPs.  
  </slide>
  <slide title="Key Safety Practices & Checklist">
    - **Pre‑start inspection**: Verify pressure gauges, safety valves, water level, and fuel supply integrity.  
    - **During operation**: Continuously monitor pressure, temperature, water level, and alarm status; log any deviations.  
    - **Post‑shutdown**: Cool‑down safely, purge residual gases, and perform visual inspection for leaks or damage.  
    - **Maintenance schedule**: Follow manufacturer‑recommended intervals for boiler tube inspections, valve testing, and water‑treatment system checks.  
    - **Documentation**: Keep accurate records of inspections, maintenance, and incident reports; review them during safety audits.  
  </slide>
</presentation>
